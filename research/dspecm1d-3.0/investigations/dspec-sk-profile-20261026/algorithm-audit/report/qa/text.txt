2. Why “two solves and multiplications” was incomplete

The adjoint formulation needs a forward field and an adjoint field for each independent objective. In
this implementation, obtaining either field means solving one radial system for each degree, family and
frequency. At L = 12, one frequency has 12 spheroidal, 12 toroidal and one radial systems. Three bins
therefore require 75 distinct dynamic matrices. The accepted Phase 1 route factors each matrix twice,
giving 150 factorizations; the prototype shares one factorization between forward and Hermitian-adjoint
substitutions. A changing frequency changes the matrix and requires new numerical factors.
There are 75,712 spatial points and six strain slots per field, so one field/frequency has 454,272 complex
strain outputs. Reconstructing those outputs also requires summing degree/order coefficients. The
profiled calculation performs about 128 million complex strain multiply-accumulates, across two
fields and three bins. The final pointwise products are cheap; the earlier explanation conflated that
cheap final work with the harmonic reconstruction.

3. What YSpec actually does differently

Inspection of SampledKernels.cpp confirms that YSpec prepares strain coefficients for all sampled
radial nodes, with degree contiguous and the real/imaginary degree dot products explicit. It groups 64
angular places in a block, constructs source/receiver Legendre tables for that block, and reuses them
over all radial nodes. Geometry is prepared before the frequency loop. The inspected version rebuilds
those block Legendre tables at each frequency; it does not already implement every cross-frequency
reuse proposed here.
YSpec constructs a basis of measurement directions and shares its radial responses and field products
across objectives. It contracts in the source frame, applying the relative source/receiver spin rotation to
the adjoint. Its radial boundary-value solver and DSpec’s assembled SEM solve are different methods,
so equal physics and output samples do not imply equal linear algebra or degrees of freedom. The
informative weighted-profile/integral comparison at the existing 10−3 tolerance passes for the established
small and large benchmark objectives. It does not certify whole-planet receiver-region strain accuracy.

5. Native SEM output and interpolation

There is no mathematical requirement to request arbitrary output radii. Native one-sided GLL traces are
a natural kernel representation. At a node, values are a gather and derivatives follow from De = Dref /Je .
Distinct element sides must remain distinct even when their radii coincide; source-lift ownership and
fluid/solid traces must not be averaged.
For arbitrary radii, storing a derivative polynomial does not make evaluating it at a new coordinate free.
The interpolation weights can be prepared once per exact element/radius and applied to all relevant
solution columns. More importantly, radial coefficient preparation is only about 6 ms of the profiled 742
ms. Removing it alone cannot explain or close the main performance gap.
The implemented bridge forms the six nodal rε numerators from U, V, W and their derivatives. For
element degree p, each numerator has degree at most p, so interpolating it and then dividing by r > 0 is
algebraically exact. Interpolating raw nodal strain directly would lose its 1/r dependence. Forming
all nodal numerators can cost more than direct evaluation for sparse output radii; it is an optional
representation with a density-dependent crossover.
Numerical boundary. Native L = 3 passed the new frozen comparison, but the L = 12 all-element
GLL output exceeded the unchanged 10−12 relative-L2 kernel gate: about 2.69 × 10−12 with Dref/J,
and 1.69 × 10−12 with cached physical-coordinate nodal derivatives. The scaled pointwise gate passed.
This is a small discrepancy, but the branch is not accepted under the current contract. The physical
derivative control reduced it without establishing a complete cause. General-radius preservation passes;
replacing the default output grid is deferred until this discrepancy is resolved.
A further dense-output representation is theoretically possible: r2 K has degree at most 2p inside an
element, so accumulate its polynomial before sampling many radii. It requires at least 2p + 1 suitable
values or coefficient products, not merely p + 1 native kernel values. The present grid has roughly eight
traces per element versus 2p + 1 = 15 at NN=8, so this is not a promising first implementation for the
measured workload. It remains relevant to much denser requested radial output.

Every same-frequency l, l′ , m, m′ pair is retained. Multiplying separately frequency-summed fields would

quality of the direct matrix implementation matter.

7. Low rank, common-frame harmonics and FFT

Each complex covariance has rank at most min(F, qA , qF ) and is generally neither Hermitian nor positive
semidefinite. The implemented thin SVD verifies candidate ranks against actual output L2 and pointwise
gates instead of choosing a singular-value cutoff. Observed ranks at 48 bins are 2–4 (L = 3), 5–7 (L = 6),
and 11–13 (L = 12). Lower factor storage does not guarantee lower total cost: SVD and verification
lose to the direct covariance route on the measured workloads.
Products of degree-L fields have scalar harmonic bandlimit 2L. A common-frame representation therefore
needs at most (2L + 1)2 = 625 coefficients at L = 12; the source-frame azimuthal restriction can reduce
that to 515. Both compact pole frames cannot simultaneously retain their small order sets after rotation
to a common frame. The prototype performs verified coefficient-frame conversion, quadrature-defined
Gaunt recoupling through 2L, and global longitude FFT synthesis. This avoids guessing phase or
normalization conventions.3
The 26 × 52 global angle grid resolves degree-24 kernel output. Gaunt and FFT coefficient comparisons
have maximum relative L2 1.94 × 10−14 and scaled pointwise 2.06 × 10−12 ; FFT and direct harmonic
synthesis agree below 1.51 × 10−13 scaled pointwise. Sparse points use covariance/direct evaluation;
FFT requires a suitable complete tensor grid.
The full recoupling plan uses numerical frame conversion and quadrature Gaunt construction, and costs
about 0.96 s at L = 12. An analytic Wigner rotation/Gaunt implementation or saved validated plan
could reduce that cost; no speedup for such an implementation has been measured. The present warm
48-bin recoupling/FFT path is comparable to covariance at L = 12. Its rough explicit payload is 154.7
MB at L12: 12.3 MB compact bases, 66.7 MB global/frame data, 75.5 MB Gaunt data, and 0.14 MB
grid addressing, excluding allocator overhead. It is a viable exact representation, not the current default
recommendation.

8. Radial solver alternatives: implemented and rejected

The SEM operator has narrow bandwidth in physical indexing (ordinary spheroidal half-bandwidth at
most about 23 for NN=8). Implemented alternatives include equilibrated pivoted general-band LU,
NaturalOrdering SparseLU with verified symbolic-pattern reuse, and element-interior static conden-
sation with full interior recovery. Forward and Hermitian-adjoint substitutions use the same factors.
Condensation uses actual LTG/interface identity, pivoted local blocks and Schur solves. It checks local
invertibility; global solvability alone does not guarantee that eliminated blocks are safe.4
The full PREM L = 12, bins 1–48 run contains 4,800 backend records. Original-system residuals
are small (forward around 5 × 10−17 , adjoint around 2 × 10−20 ), but this does not establish output
equivalence. The changed elimination orders fail the frozen field/contact gates:

unchanged production evaluator: returned raw/processed spectra, objectives and all six stored modulus
channels, at exact matched coordinates. Repeated outputs are deterministic. Maximum kernel relative
L2 is 8.12 × 10−14 and scaled pointwise 9.04 × 10−12 . Expanded tests separately establish 336 public
field and 240 source-contact comparisons per bridge setting against the saved production arrays. An
additional complete permuted PREM tensor product agrees with the standard field reconstruction in

The response-cache contacts-on and native L12 failures remain explicitly rejected; all gates were retained.

10. Disposition and concrete adoption sequence

   1. Promote semantic angular planning and tiled direct synthesis first. Keep the existing
      equilibrated factorization and arbitrary-radius API. Integrate one shared factor per matrix, exact
      receiver basis handling, persistent grid/frame plans and the measured best real/complex matrix
      layout. Preserve the packed fallback and strict contact path. Repeat the existing production
      regression gates on the integrated change.

   2. Integrate objective reuse with separate contact rules. Preserve each full P ∗ action. Forward
      angular-field reuse has passed contact-bearing checks with the original weighted solves. The batch
      response cache saves repeated radial solves, but must gain an explicit contact-safe route and robust
      bounded-cache fallback before becoming a general API default.

   3. Offer exact frequency covariance for sufficiently many bins. Integrate the proven covariance
      algorithm behind a measured direct/covariance dispatch; measure the complete solver-to-output
      path and tile coefficient history by radial blocks. The standalone warm advantage is established; a
      full-grid end-to-end gain is not yet measured.

   4. Retain native SEM output as a deliberate feature. Resolve the strict L = 12 native-node
      discrepancy before making it the default. Retain optional arbitrary-radius resampling and side
      provenance. Do not spend the main performance effort on interpolation alone.

No approximate degree decoupling, cross-frequency product, unverified rank truncation, Hermitian-
positive-definite solver, raw-strain interpolation, or negative-order real-field symmetry is a valid shortcut
here. GPU and multi-thread designs need a separately authorized hardware/resource comparison; this
audit supplies the single-thread algorithmic baseline. Strict-IEEE architecture-specific compiler tuning
is a useful secondary control, rather than a substitute for the demonstrated data reuse. No fast-math
flags were used.
Coverage boundary. Promising mathematical families were derived and runnable prototypes were
exercised before disposition. This is not a claim that every possible implementation or hardware backend
has been exhausted. Receiver-region physical limitations, mesh/degree convergence, general attenua-
tion/measurement combinations and large receiver ensembles require their own validation. Production
numerical code remains the accepted Phase 1 version; the audit delivers concrete implementations,
preserved failures and a measured integration order.
