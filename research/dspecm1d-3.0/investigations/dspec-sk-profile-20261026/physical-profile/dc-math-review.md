# Read-only mathematical check: DC and physical windowed vertical energy

This is an implementation-facing theory check before the parent runs physical convergence cases. I read the 2026-10-26 protocol, checked decisions, synthetic measurement probe, and production DSpecM1D/YSpecpp source. I did not compile, run a numerical solve, edit production code, or validate a PREM waveform or kernel. A final independent review must use the resulting evidence.

## Explicit frequency and DC policy

- At `dt=5 s`, requested output duration `3600 s`, and decay 5, both grids yield `N=1024`, FFT period `5120 s`, `df=1/5120 Hz`, and `epsilon=5/3600 s^-1`. DSpec `FreqFull::i1()` and YSpec `FrequencyGrid::First()` both clamp the computed first bin to **1** even when the requested minimum is zero. Thus neither default high-level spectrum contains DC. DSpec's `i2()` is exclusive and gives 53; YSpec's `Last()` is inclusive and gives 52. Both include the guard bin `k=52` at 10.15625 mHz. Bins `k=0..51` are at or below 10 mHz.
- Compute `k=0` explicitly at the **complex contour frequency** `z=omega_0-i epsilon=-i epsilon`, with the same source, full-gravity setting, source/receiver geometry, and degree range as other bins. DSpec's high-level 3D forward/field interfaces accept `firstBin=0` and use `z=w[k]-i ep`; the production default `i1` should not be mistaken for this explicit path. YSpec's public `MakeGrid`/`SeismogramSpectra` cannot reach `k=0` because `FrequencyGrid::First()` is clamped; its isolated adapter needs an explicit low-level DC solve and insertion into the padded one-sided spectrum (or an equivalently audited isolated grid change). `Omega(0)` on the default YSpec grid is **k=1**, not DC.
- A step moment-tensor source is common to these paths. DSpec displacement uses `outputFactor(0,z)=-i/z=1/(i z)` after its impulsive spatial source; YSpec `SourceWeights::Factor` includes `1/(i omega)` for the step source. At `z=-i epsilon`, this is finite (`1/epsilon`). Do not replace it with an undamped static `omega=0` solve, silently zero DC, or apply an extra derivative/impulse conversion. YSpec's public `Source` documents a step at origin time; compare the same nine tensor entries and SI-to-model normalization rather than presuming equal amplitudes from code shape alone.
- Purely imaginary `z` makes `z^2` and, with attenuation off, the material operator real. A real physical receiver component should therefore have real DC after the angular/order sum, up to roundoff. The real inverse FFT necessarily projects away imaginary DC and Nyquist endpoint parts. Save the raw complex endpoints, their imaginary-to-real residuals, and the projected values. Accept numerical projection only if the residual is negligible relative to a documented spectral scale; a material imaginary component means the DC solve or phase convention is wrong. The finite `epsilon` removes the algebraic `1/z` singularity, but does **not** prove all modal solves are stable: check finite values and solver residuals/conditioning for radial `l=0`, spheroidal `l=1` (translation/static gauge sensitivity), and the fluid/gravity blocks. In YSpec, `Context` has finite `1/omega^2` at this DC, and its turning-point start uses the bottom for `omega.real()==0`; that is a defined route, not a convergence proof.
- The 8–10 mHz raised-cosine band weight is 1 below 8 mHz, `0.5[1+cos(pi(f_mHz-8)/2)]` on 8–10 mHz, and zero from 10 mHz upward. Apply it to the **raw damped-contour complex spectra**, including DC, before inverse FFT, undoing damping, then applying `sqrt(Hann[1000,1200]`) and the final transform. YSpec's `Taper{0,0,0.008,0.010}` has this shape; DSpec's adapter must apply the identical explicit weights. This is a well-defined contour-sampled, postprocessed observable. Because the transfer functions are evaluated at `omega-i epsilon`, it is not automatically the ideal sharp band of an undamped real-axis Fourier spectrum. Moving the filter after damping removal changes the operation.

## Matched observable and adjoint factors

Let `B` be the real diagonal contour-bin taper and `P` map the padded raw nondimensional spectrum to the SI Fourier transform of the undamped, `sqrt(Hann)`-windowed vertical displacement. With one-sided weights `eta_0=eta_Nyquist=1`, `eta_k=2` otherwise, the target is

`E = (df/2) sum_k eta_k |(P B x)_k|^2 = (dt_SI/2) sum_n Hann_n u_z,n^2`.

For a frozen baseline `x`, set `q_k=df eta_k (P B x)_k` and use the raw-spectrum gradient `B P* q`, with `P*` the exact real-inner-product adjoint. Then `Re<q,P B x>=2E` and `delta E=Re<B P* q,delta x>`. The synthetic `measurement_probe.cpp` establishes Parseval, `P/P*`, and a finite-difference check for this construction only; it does not establish a physical PREM signal. The window mixes frequencies: retain the full padded vector for `P` and `P*`, and apply `B` again on the adjoint path before the per-bin kernel solves. Computing kernels only for bins with nonzero `B` is valid after that final multiplication; truncating `P*` to the band before applying it is not.

For YSpec's `Measurement::adjointSource`, supply `j_n=Hann_n u_z,n` in SI metres for this energy, frozen from the same filtered baseline. `MakeAdjointSpectrum` itself sums `j_n` with the nondimensional `dt` and scales by `Unit*Time`; including `dt` in `j` would double count it. Check the custom DC adjoint frequency has one-sided weight `df` rather than `2df`; all interior frequencies get `2df` and Nyquist gets `df` if included. Compare its returned linear `q·Pu` (which is `2E` at the baseline) separately from `E`.

YSpec's `Calculation` defaults to attenuation **on**, while the inspected DSpec 3D field path rejects attenuation. Set both to attenuation off, full gravity, displacement, and no instrument. YSpec's raw `SeismogramSpectra` is nondimensional and untapered; `InverseTransform` inserts its taper, multiplies by grid spacing, undoes `exp(-epsilon t)`, and `ComputeSeismograms` then multiplies by the model's displacement unit. DSpec's raw spectrum and `WindowedDisplacementMap` use the analogous inverse-spacing/undamping and length scale, with `dt_SI` on the final transform. Compare both raw spectra, the filtered SI vertical trace, and energy before using a common `j`; do not repair an amplitude/sign discrepancy by renormalizing one trace.

The protocol's `(10°,20°)` source and `(45°,75°)` receiver latitudes are **geocentric**. DSpec's 3D pole frame uses input latitude directly. YSpec `Location` is geographic and `Sphere::Colatitude` applies `tan(phi_geocentric)=0.993277 tan(phi_geographic)`. Convert each protocol latitude with `phi_geographic=atan(tan(phi_geocentric)/0.993277)` before constructing YSpec source/receiver; retain both coordinate sets in run metadata. The vertical component is radial/up in both APIs; verify tensor component order and signs at the input boundary.

## Convergence order and bounded kernel grid

1. Establish a nonzero, finite baseline vertical trace in the 1000–1200 s window at a degree sufficient to contain the arrival, and save the raw DC residual, filtered trace, energy, and `q·Pu/2E` identity. The TauP arrivals in the checked decisions support the window location only at ray level.
2. Keep the *defined target contour* fixed at `epsilon=5/3600 s^-1`. Increase DSpec `qex` from 1 to 2 at `dt=5 s`, which increases `N` from 1024 to 2048 while preserving `epsilon`, the source, band, and window. YSpec's `FrequencyGrid` computes `N` from `epsilon/(2*pi)` and `dt` alone; changing `length=3600`/`decay=5` to `length=7200`/`decay=10` leaves `epsilon` **and N** unchanged. A comparable YSpec diagnostic therefore needs an isolated explicit-`N` or `qex` grid override that halves the tentative spacing used to choose `N` while keeping `epsilon` fixed, or an equivalent audited explicit frequency array/inverse transform. Compare traces on common 1000–1200 s samples and energy using the protocol's fixed relative gates. This tests frequency-spacing/FFT-period effects for the same contour observable.
3. Refine DSpec radial SEM resolution and YSpec radial integration tolerance independently, then increase `Lmax` through the approved candidates (64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 384 as needed) until waveform and energy satisfy the fixed `1e-3` gates against a higher-resolution reference. A single adjacent agreement can be accidental, so retain at least one further refinement where feasible. After both solvers are internally stable, apply the `1e-3` cross-code trace and energy gates at the matched geometry and settings. Keep failures and the actual resource ceiling visible.
4. Separately vary contour damping (for example, duration 7200 s with default decay 5, which halves `epsilon`) and report its effect as **damping/contour-filter sensitivity**. It changes the raw frequency response and thus the filtered observable, so do not call its difference solely FFT aliasing or padding error.
5. Only after waveform/energy convergence, plan a complete-`2L` angular kernel grid on explicitly chosen native SEM radial sides. The checked 56-trace native-grid fixture spans 4,200–6,200 km; proposed source and receiver are at 6,321 and 6,368 km. Reusing it measures a **partial radial domain** and omits source/receiver neighbourhoods. Full Earth kernel claims need a stated radial domain, all relevant one-sided interface traces and quadrature, and treatment/exclusion accounting for singular source/receiver regions. A complete angular grid alone does not supply these.

The number of angular nodes for `ForBand(L,2,2.0)` is at least `(2L+1)(4L+1)` before fast-longitude rounding. At `L=128`, that is at least 131,841 nodes per radial trace, or 7.38 million rows for the existing 56 traces; at `L=192` it is at least 296,065 nodes per trace, or 16.58 million rows. The saved L12 CSV's roughly 106 bytes/row suggests about 0.8 GB and 1.8 GB of CSV respectively, before a larger full-radius domain, parsed point arrays, per-bin fields, basis/cache copies, and output. Under an 8 GB address-space limit, stream/chunk and record peak memory; make a measured plan before scheduling a realistic-`L` kernel run. If only a bounded radial sample is feasible, name that limited product precisely and do not infer a complete Earth-kernel runtime.
